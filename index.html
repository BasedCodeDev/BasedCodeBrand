01 / Essence

What we mean

Definition and positioning

BasedCode is Seb Fehr’s creator and technology brand. It turns real game and software production into useful, entertaining content through live development, technical explanation, honest experiments, and visible outcomes.

Unlike generic technology commentary or performative “build in public” content, BasedCode is grounded in active projects, working code, production experience, and conclusions supported by evidence.

The brand is not a course, consultancy, professional-services offer, or performance of expertise. It is a living record of making.

Audience

Practising and aspiring developers, indie teams and technical founders, and technology-curious viewers who want genuine access to active work and a place to compare notes, ask questions, and make progress with others.

Content pillars

BUILD software, games, tools, and systems.
PLAY test ideas, games, experiments, and weird edges.
LEARN share lessons, failures, discoveries, and context.
TOGETHER invite feedback, playtests, questions, and shared momentum.

Brand shorthand

Experienced builder.
Curious experimenter.
Candid host.
Generous collaborator.

Curious, capable, playful, dynamic, practical, transparent, and welcoming.

Strategic foundation

PURPOSE

Make ambitious creative technology understandable by showing the real work, trade-offs, failures, and decisions involved in building it.

MISSION

Build worthwhile games and software in public, give people meaningful access to the process, and create a place where practical knowledge and momentum are shared.

VISION

A creator-led technical community where entertainment, education, real production, and peer contribution reinforce each other from concept through release and revision.

PROMISE

You will see real work, real constraints, honest conclusions, and people making progress together.

Core principles

Build real things

Start from genuine production, tests, releases, failures, or decisions wherever possible.

Show the reasoning

Explain constraints, alternatives, trade-offs, consequences, and why a decision was made.

Tools, not religion

Evaluate AI and emerging technology through real use, including clean-up, limitations, integration, and cost.

Respect intelligence

Translate complexity without trivialising it or using inflated hooks that misrepresent the result.

Bound participation

Let the community influence experiments and playtests without surrendering product coherence or scope.

Complete the loop

Show concept, implementation, release, response, evaluation, and improvement—not only the exciting beginning.

03 / Voice

Human, useful, alive

Core voice

BasedCode speaks in a direct, intelligent, first-person voice: an experienced developer thinking clearly in public and making room for other people’s insight.

Direct Lead with the useful point and prefer concrete language over filler.
Evidence-led Tie conclusions to tests, implementation, observed results, or clearly labelled judgement.
Technically accessible Use correct terminology, then explain why it matters.
Candid Separate facts, assumptions, and open questions without pretending to have every answer.
Generous Credit contributions, respond to real questions, and make useful knowledge easy to pass on.
Earned energy Be excited when a release, result, milestone, or community achievement gives a concrete reason.

Use

Plain language. Specific examples. Honest constraints and trade-offs. Warmth, patient explanation, natural humour, and occasional profanity. “I don’t know yet” is allowed.

Avoid

Fake certainty, income promises, hustle clichés, empty AI hype, generic enthusiasm, targeted jokes, and claims detached from evidence.

Vocabulary guidance

Favour

Build, test, ship, revise, practical, working, playable, measurable, constraint, trade-off, scope, implementation, experiment, result, finding, limitation, live, behind the scenes, milestone, release, and post-mortem.

Purple carries the signal.

Use

Build fields from plum, deep purple, and core purple. Use violet and lumen as illumination, hierarchy, and moving energy. White is the key colour on dark and purple fields. Light applications use a warm near-white canvas, deep-plum text, and the same purple hierarchy.

Avoid

Competing accent colours, green, red, blue-violet neon, rainbow palettes, arbitrary glow, or flat purple slabs. Create emphasis through contrast, scale, weight, opacity, and motion.

Motion language

BasedCode should feel alive wherever the medium supports it. Motion is smooth, subtle, and continuous: usually noticed as polish before it is noticed as animation. Every gradient must move on a motion-capable surface; a static gradient is only a fallback when motion is explicitly paused or the format is inherently static. Gradient movement must become perceptible within two to three seconds. Page-level ambient gradient movement belongs inside the hero; the document body remains a stable dark workspace. BasedCode pages use Text Ripple JS as a signature transition for short, prominent text.

DO

Animate the hero gradient and intentional component gradients with visible but smooth colour-field travel. Ripple hero statements, section titles, compact labels, and occasional live-state text.

DON’T

Animate the full-page document background. Do not make movement so slow that it reads as static, ripple paragraphs continuously, or use random glitch, jitter, and gamer-RGB effects.

USE WHEN

Use text ripple for arrival, reveal, state change, or intentional interaction. Preserve the original text during repeat effects and provide an explicit control to pause all motion.

07 / Imagery

Show the work

Preferred image world

Real screens. Half-finished prototypes. Game builds. Desk setups. Stream moments. Event rooms. Code in context. The image should feel close enough to touch, not like stock technology.

LIVE BUILD / NOT MONSTERS 09:42:18
The bug is real.
The fix is probably worse.

Composition

Prefer strong crops, clear focal points, visible texture, and a deliberate relationship between image and type. Use overlays to add information, not to hide weak imagery.

USE WHEN A real moment can communicate the process faster than an explanation.

Visual guardrails

DO Show Seb actively building, testing, presenting, or directing work. Keep visible game, code, art, diagram, prototype, and before/after context. Generated support imagery must feel believable and authored.
DON’T Use hooded hackers, Matrix code, generic cyberpunk, esports aggression, fake product screenshots, decorative terminal clichés, or inconsistent AI imagery as a substitute for a system. Never let the medieval or horror-comedy aesthetic of Not Monsters become the master BasedCode identity.
